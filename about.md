## Biography

Dr Benjamin Atta Owusu is a health data scientist and epidemiologist specialising in multimorbidity, cardiovascular and respiratory epidemiology, and interpretable machine learning. He is a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Exeter Collaboration for Academic Primary Care (APEx), University of Exeter Medical School, and a Graduate Statistician (GradStat) with the Royal Statistical Society.

Dr Owusu joined the University of Exeter in December 2023. Before that, he was a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Faculty of Medicine at Prince of Songkla University, Thailand, where he modelled COVID-19 mortality and longitudinal critical-care outcomes, and a Research Assistant in the university's Division of Digital Innovation and Data Analytics. He studied mathematics at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Ghana, then completed an MSc in applied mathematics and a PhD in research methodology at Prince of Songkla University. His research experience spans Ghana, Thailand and the United Kingdom.

His research follows people living with heart failure and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD): the time between diagnoses of the two conditions, the long-term conditions that accumulate alongside them, respiratory infections and antibiotic prescribing, and how outcomes differ between patient groups. He works with routinely collected linked health data, connecting primary care records with hospital admissions, death registrations, prescribing and area deprivation. His methods include survival and competing-risks analysis, longitudinal mixed-effects models, and interpretable machine learning.

Dr Owusu leads stakeholder-informed work that turns prediction models into tools clinicians can use. Working with healthcare professionals, patients and carers, he developed a seven-stage framework that brings human-computer interaction methods into each stage of building a machine-learning model, using falls prediction in heart failure as the worked example. He was awarded an EPSRC LEAP Digital Health Hub Solo Fellowship (2024) to predict falls and hip fractures from routinely collected care data, and is a co-applicant on an HDR UK South West seedcorn award (2025) on data visualisation for inequalities in cancer and cardiovascular disease.

He is committed to building research capacity. He supervises MSc and PhD students in health data science at the University of Exeter, teaches medical students on the patient experience of multimorbidity, and mentored two master's students in Thailand whose projects were published.
